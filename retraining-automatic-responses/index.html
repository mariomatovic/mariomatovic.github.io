Experimental method

RE-TRAIN AUTOMATIC RESPONSES

An experimental approach to changing how learned automatic responses behave under pressure, especially when fear, uncertainty, or overload make conscious control less reliable.

Much of psychological self-improvement works at the level of thoughts: analyze them, challenge them, replace them, or try to think differently.

That can be useful. But performance is often decided one layer deeper—by the responses the brain has already learned and can produce before deliberate thought has time to intervene.

My interest is in that gap: how can an athlete train the automatic system itself, rather than relying on another thought to control it in the moment?

CORE IDEAThe objective is not to give an athlete more instructions to remember under pressure. It is to develop responses that become increasingly available without excessive conscious interference.

Where it matters

  1. 01Pressure: when the athlete knows what to do but an automatic response gets in the way.
  2. 02Performance: when too much conscious control interferes with skills that are already trained.
  3. 03Development: when the goal is to change a response pattern, not simply manage the moment.
THE WORK

The method explores whether specific mental states and responses can be trained more directly, so that the athlete does not have to consciously manufacture them every time.

The exact training process is part of the ongoing development and testing of the method. What matters here is the principle: change the response, not just the commentary around it.

In practice, this is where performance psychology becomes less about collecting techniques and more about deliberately shaping what happens when the moment becomes difficult.

Examples of target states
01 / NEUTRAL

NEUTRAL

Reducing unnecessary mental activity and returning attention to what is actually happening.

A useful reference state for athletes who become overloaded by internal noise.

02 / RELAX

RELAX

Reducing unnecessary physical tension without losing readiness or intensity.

Relevant when pressure changes the athlete’s movement, timing, or normal execution.

03 / COURAGE

COURAGE

Maintaining commitment to the task when uncertainty or fear is present.

Not the absence of fear—the ability to keep behavior aligned with the objective.
