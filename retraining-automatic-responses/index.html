A NEW WAY TO TRAIN AUTOMATIC RESPONSES

RETRAINING
AUTOMATIC RESPONSES

We usually try to change our mind by thinking about our thoughts. This method explores something more direct: issue a command and allow the automatic system to execute it.

Your brain constantly runs automatic processes based on what you have learned — skills, experiences, expectations, emotions, habits and responses to situations.

Under pressure, fear or uncertainty, many responses happen automatically before conscious thinking can control them.

We often try to change those responses by thinking: “I need to relax.” “I shouldn't be anxious.” “I need to be confident.” But the underlying system is still producing the response.

Think about reaching for a glass. You don't calculate every movement or verbally instruct your arm and fingers. You simply intend to take it.

This method explores whether the same direct relationship can be deliberately used for psychological states and behavior: directly intend the desired state, then allow the automatic system to execute it.

The working idea: repeated direct access to a desired state may retrain the learned automatic response. This is what I am testing.

01—04

THE FIRST COMMANDS

These are the first controls to test.
Don't think the state. Intend it.

01

NEUTRAL

No thoughts. No mental processing. Nothing to do.

When you reach for a glass, you don't think about how to do it. You simply intend to take it.

Use the same principle with your mind. Don't fight thoughts. Don't replace them. Don't analyze.

INTENDSIMPLY BE.
02

RELAX

Every muscle releases unnecessary tension at the same time.

Briefly picture your entire body completely relaxed.

Then simply intend that state to happen.

INTENDCOMPLETELY RELAXED.
03

CONFIDENT

I trust my ability to handle what is in front of me.

Don't think about confidence or convince yourself. Directly intend the state of trusting your own ability.

INTENDI CAN HANDLE THIS.
04

COURAGE

Fear can be present, but it does not control action.

The target is complete commitment toward the intended goal despite fear.

INTENDI'M GOING.
