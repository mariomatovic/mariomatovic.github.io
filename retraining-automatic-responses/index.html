A different starting point

RE-TRAIN AUTOMATIC RESPONSES

An experimental approach to how learned automatic responses behave under pressure, especially when fear, uncertainty, or overload make conscious control less reliable.

The usual route

Most existing approaches stay focused on thought processes: from “I shouldn’t be anxious” and “I need to relax” to challenging, accepting, reframing, or trying not to believe thoughts.

The question here

What if we are usually talking to the brain indirectly? It can feel like standing next to a car while someone else drives, trying to persuade them which way to turn.

The principle

This work explores a more direct, non-verbal command: holding a clear target in mind and directing the brain toward it. The full training process remains part of the method itself.

A simple example

If a glass of water is in front of you and you decide to pick it up, you do not need a conversation about how to move your hand. The target is there, and the mind carries out the action. This is the basic mechanism the method is interested in.

What is shown here: the principle and a few target states. The complete training sequence, timing, and applied process are deliberately not published on this page.
